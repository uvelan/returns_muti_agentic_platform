### The fix

- **New `operations/support_template_draft.py`** â€” the seam, and the vocabulary's
  one home. It declares `SNAPSHOT_KEYS` (names the draft derives, no `factId`),
  `FACT_LOG_KEYS` (names an activity already writes), their union, and the
  conditional wording as named constants. `support_template_snapshot(...)` takes
  **`compose_support_handoff`'s own argument list** â€” deliberately, so a caller
  that can compose one can produce the other without deciding anything new, and
  so the equivalence matrix can drive both paths from one set of inputs.
- **The conditionals resolve to presence, not to a fallback.** The Â§8 clause
  grammar describes the shape of a *case*, never the presence of a value, so no
  `visibility_rule` can express "only when the bay was not recommended". Those
  lines are bound with **no `fallback`**: the producer omits the key and the
  renderer omits the line, exactly as the composed path omits it. A fallback
  there is what printed "Not available" where the truth was "no bay could be
  recommended, resolve manually".
- **The contact block is two mutually exclusive arms**, six fields, three ever
  present. Labels stay in configuration (the alternative â€” one pre-rendered
  block â€” would have taken the wording out of the operator's hands); it is the
  *arm* that is chosen in code, because the arm is the conditional.
- **`return_details.additional`** renders through one key holding already-
  labelled lines, because the labels are the release's and no template can know
  them. That preserves `support_handoff.py:157-160`'s property: a release that
  adds a required detail needs neither module edited.
- **`production.yaml`**: the customer, return-details, bay, verification and
  requested-action sections gain their conditional fields; `associate_notes`
  rebinds to `associate_notes_rendered`; the block comment now states what is
  claimed and what is not.
- **F2, the second copy:** `api/template_preview.py` held a hand-written second
  copy of the whole 24-key vocabulary. Deleted. The sample is `SAMPLE_CASE` in
  the seam module, expressed as compose arguments, and its facts come from
  `draft_facts` â€” so the route's "the same text as `compose_support_handoff`"
  claim is now `test_the_preview_is_the_composed_text`, which posts the
  **shipped** default variant and compares.

original. `test_an_edited_field_changes_the_body` asserts exactly that gap.

### One design change I made mid-step, and why

I first had the workflow derive the request ids and loop `record_template_draft`
per request. That put `one_per_case` in the workflow and quietly collapsed the
other two groupings — which would have made "a held record must not block an
approved one" **unfalsifiable**, because the gate could only ever produce one
request. The DoD asks for that test by name, so the design was wrong, not the
test.

`record_template_draft` now resolves the grouping activity-side (it reads
return records, which a workflow may not) and returns a `TemplateReviewDraftSet`
— the whole map in one result. Two consequences worth stating: the ids the wait
is keyed on are **in the history**, not re-derived from a release that may have
moved; and replay safety comes from `create_review`'s documented idempotency on
`(case_id, request_id, kind, scope)` rather than from a replay-stable uuid.

### The wait loop

Cloned from `_await_policy_override` — resumed deadline, `resolve_business_deadline`
legs, reminder cap, `continue_as_new` on the history hint — and a clone by
ruling 12, not by accident; the extraction stays registered.

Two deliberate differences from the source, both recorded:

1. `_await_policy_override` waits for *one* answer and returns on the first
   wake. This drains a notice list and continues, because a case with two
   requests must be able to send the approved one while the other is still
   being read.
2. At the reminder cap the policy loop **returns**; this one keeps waiting to
   the deadline. Stopping at the cap is the reminder count deciding the
   deadline, and a draft is still perfectly answerable after the last reminder.
   The single exit stays in the `remaining <= 0` branch.

**No reminder is sent on Channel B, and that is the gate.** `send_support_reminder`
posts on the case's Support thread and during this wait there is no such thread
— refusing to open one until a person has read the draft is the entire feature,
so nudging Support that our reviewer has not looked yet would be the gate
opening the conversation the gate is holding back. `_await_policy_override`
states the same rule for the same reason. The cadence is durable where it is
actually read: the count rides `continue_as_new` and reaches the panel through
`execution_state`, and the deadline is an absolute instant the browser counts
down from (§9).

### Deadline policy, and the one contract reading I had to make

`hold` and `escalate` park the case and leave every review `OPEN`: a deadline
passing does not make a draft un-reviewable, and a reviewer arriving late can
still answer. `auto_send` approves as `SYSTEM` through the *same* transition
with the same refusals.

**The gap rule is enforced twice**, in the workflow and again in the activity,
and the duplication is deliberate: it is the one rule in the gate whose failure
mode is a message asserting something the case does not know, to a human who
then acts on it. The workflow's copy is a decision; the activity's is a refusal.

*Reading recorded for RV:* `HELD_FOR_OPERATIONS` is reachable only from
`APPROVING` in S2's frozen table, so a guard-blocked review that never left
`OPEN` cannot enter it. The guard block is therefore recorded as the **case**
park reason `TEMPLATE_REVIEW_GUARD_BLOCKED` (a §6 park reason) with the review
left `OPEN` and answerable. The alternative — approving a gapped draft purely
to make the hold transition available — would freeze a payload the case cannot
support in order to satisfy a state diagram.

### F-3 â€” revise, cancel and redraft were dead ends

`ReviewAggregateStore.cancel`, `request_revision` and `redraft` record **no
command** â€” only `approve` and `retry_delivery` do, because those two need one
inside their own transaction for the frozen CAS. So the abandoned endpoints
moved the review in Mongo and told the execution nothing:

* **revise** set `pendingRevision`, which blocks approval, and nothing ever
  produced the re-render it blocks for. The review was permanently unapprovable.
* **cancel** moved the review to `CANCELLED` while the wait map still held it
  `OPEN`; the case parked `TEMPLATE_REVIEW_UNANSWERED` at its deadline instead
  of `TEMPLATE_REVIEW_CANCELLED` immediately.
* **redraft** cancelled one attempt and minted another with a **new**
  `review_id`; the map held the old one, so every later decision about the
  request was discarded as "not this case's open attempt". Redraft was
  completely unreachable.

The three now record durable commands through
`DurableCaseCommandStore.record_command` (sect. 7: REST never signals Temporal
directly). `note` finally travels â€” `record_template_revision` already took one
and neutralised it onto the fact log, and was being handed `None` forever.

**Signal ids are deterministic**, not uuids: `template_cancelled:{review_id}`
and `template_revised:{review_id}:{draft_version}`. One revision per version of
a draft is exactly the rule, and it makes the endpoints re-entrant across the
two-transaction window.

**The window, stated rather than hidden.** The state move and the command are
two transactions, because the aggregate's moves go through `_guarded_update`
which owns its own. State first: if the command write fails, the case waits to
its deadline and parks as unanswered with the review's real state on the panel
throughout. The other ordering fails worse â€” for `cancel` it would leave the
execution holding a review it would happily send.

**`supersedes`** is the redraft fix, and it is deliberately narrow: a revision
notice may re-point the map only when it names the attempt the workflow is
*currently holding*. A notice that merely disagrees with the map still gets
discarded, which is the multi-RMA property the original guard existed for.

recover nothing but the status, and the panel's only possible answer to every
refusal would be "try again" â€” which is precisely the answer sect. 6 says is
not good enough. Populated from the same payload `extractErrorDetails` already
reads, so no new parse and no new failure mode.

### The ETag client

`apiClient` **cannot** serve the panel and that is the whole reason
`readCasePanel` exists: it reads the JSON body unconditionally, and a 304 has no
body, so the shared client throws "Unable to read the API response" on the
*successful* half of the mechanism. The panel read therefore uses `fetch`
directly and keeps a module-scoped `{etag, view}` per case.

Module-scoped rather than per-caller because a 304 is only useful to something
that kept the bytes. One case has one panel and every viewer of it in this tab
is looking at the same thing â€” which is exactly the principal-independence the
shared payload rests on, so one entry per case is correct rather than merely
convenient.

The 304-with-nothing-cached branch retries **unconditionally**. It is a real
state (the module dropped an entry the browser still validates against) and the
alternative is handing a caller `undefined` and blanking the panel. The retry
sends no validator, so it cannot loop.

`panelRefetchInterval` stops on a 4xx for `caseRefetchInterval`'s reason and
**does not** stop when every review is terminal â€” the workflow is still running
and its timers still move, and stopping there would freeze the screen at the
moment the associate handed off. Asserted in both directions.

### The mocks are stateful, deliberately

A panel whose handler returned the same body forever lets a developer look at
the screen and nothing else: approving appears to do nothing, the conflict
banner never clears, and the autosave/restore path â€” most of what an associate
actually does here â€” cannot be walked. So the mutations mutate a store and the
panel composes from it, as the backend composes from Mongo.

The mock ETag is derived from the composed body, not from a mutation counter.
A counter moves on a write that changed nothing, which would make the 304
branch unreachable and the mock would be exercising the half that does not
matter.

### Rule 1 â€” the retry refuses instead of stranding

`execution_holds_review` asks the **execution**, not the aggregate. The
aggregate says what state a review is in; only the execution knows whether
anything is still listening for a decision about it, and a retry that satisfies
the first and not the second is exactly the one that strands the review.
`execution_state` already carries `template_reviews`, because the panel composes
from it â€” no new query, no new field.

**The check runs before the CAS, not after.** `retry_delivery` moves the state
*and* records the command in one transaction; a guard after it would be a
refusal reporting a change it had already made.

Two refusals, not one, and the distinction is the point:

- **409 `ExecutionNoLongerHoldingReview`** â€” definitively not held. Carries
  `state: HELD_FOR_OPERATIONS` and names the legal action ("Reopen the review
  â€¦ or stop trying and record why"), which is the whole reason to refuse here
  rather than succeed into a discard.
- **503 `EXECUTION_LIVENESS_UNKNOWN`** â€” no Temporal client, or the host would
  not answer. Fails closed either way, but telling an operator "this is final"
  when the truth is "ask again in a minute" would send them to abandon a
  message that was still perfectly deliverable.

### Rule 2 â€” the gate parks what it was holding

`hold_unsettled` moves **every** non-terminal review on close. Not only
`APPROVING`: an `OPEN` review after the gate has closed is *the same trap
through the approve endpoint* â€” approving CASes it to `APPROVING`, the workflow
discards the notice, and those three exits are all workflow-driven. Moving it
out shuts that door rather than guarding it, which is why the amendment says
every non-terminal review and why a test covers the `OPEN` case specifically.

**A `continue_as_new` is not a close.** The next run re-enters the gate holding
the same reviews, and `HELD_FOR_OPERATIONS` is a *resolved* state â€” parking
there would make the resumed run find everything settled and send nothing, for a
case nobody had answered. A `continuing` flag set immediately before
`workflow.continue_as_new` is what the `finally` checks, so every other exit,
including an exception, is a real close.

The hold is **best-effort by design**: it runs in a `finally`, so raising there
would replace whatever was already unwinding â€” including a cancellation â€” with
an activity error, and the case would lose the reason it was ending. A review
left unparked is visible on the panel and parked by a later close; a swallowed
cancellation is not.

### Ownership: an S2-owned file, changed deliberately

`_TRANSITIONS` gains `OPEN -> HELD_FOR_OPERATIONS` and
`DELIVERY_FAILED -> HELD_FOR_OPERATIONS`, and `hold_for_operations` accepts those
two states. Â§10 gives S2 the state transitions, so **this is V1 editing an
S2-owned file and it is called out rather than slipped in.** It is done here

deliberately **not** added: re-holding would overwrite the first hold reason with
a later one, and the caller treats an already-held review as the no-op it is.

### RV advisory A1 â€” taken, and the reasoning that decides which endpoints get it

`retry_review_delivery` now uses `review_delivery_retry:{review_id}`.

**`approve` keeps its random id, and that is not an oversight.** A deterministic

re-driving one delivery, where a repeat is the *same* decision and must be
recognised. Deterministic where a repeat means "again", random where a repeat
means "somebody else".

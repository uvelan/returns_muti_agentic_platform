## Finding

### F1 (BLOCKING) — `mentionsSnakeCaseKeys` inspects one level; the readers descend two, and the uninspected level is where the artifacts are

- **Files:** `frontend/src/domains/returns/panes/casePanel/support/supportPanelPayloads.ts:137-145` (the detector) against `:376` and `:394` (`readObjects(entry, "artifacts")`, a depth-2 descent).
- **Rule:** the amendment's reporting half — AMENDMENT-7 forbids a tolerant reader precisely so disagreement is *observable*; a strict reader that draws an empty section without a notice is the invisibility AMENDMENT-6 was raised about, one level deeper.
- **Confirmed by RV, directly.** Handing the detector and the reader a payload that is correct at the root and at `records[]` but snake_case inside `records[].artifacts[]` — which is the shipped shape, `supportHandlers.ts:137-150` emits `records[] → {returnRecordId, artifacts[]}` with artifact elements `{artifactType, value}`:

  ```
  depth0Detected (nl_enabled at the root)            : true
  depth1Detected (return_record_id inside records[]) : true
  depth2Detected (artifact_type inside artifacts[])  : false
  readerProducedArtifacts                            : []
  ```

  The reader is correctly strict — it drops the artifact rather than translating it — and the detector says nothing. The card renders for that return with **zero artifacts and no notice**, which reads exactly like "Support has attached nothing to this return."
- **Scope, checked precisely so the fix is small.** I enumerated every descent the readers make: `records[]`, `unbound[]`, `messages[]` and `placement` are all depth-1 and covered; **`records[].artifacts[]` is the only depth-2 site**, and it carries the artifact type and value — the substantive content of the return-records card, and the thing a person acts on.
- **Why it is blocking rather than an advisory.** The whole argument for removing the dual-read is that a strict reader plus a notice is better than a tolerant one, because the disagreement becomes visible. At depth 2 the branch gets the strictness without the visibility, which is the weaker half of the old behaviour rather than the stronger half of the new one. The design decision in item 2 is right; this is that decision not reaching far enough.
- **Fix.** Recurse in `mentionsSnakeCaseKeys` with a small depth cap (two is enough for every shape in use; three would cost nothing), or special-case the one known depth-2 array. Add the depth-2 case to `is reported rather than drawn as an absence`, which currently pins depth 0 and depth 1 only.

## The AMENDMENT-7 enforcement test — verified, and the blindness claim is sound

This is the most interesting thing on the branch and I probed it hardest.

**The behavioural guard is load-bearing.** Three separate tolerance injections, each reverted:

| injection | full support suite | with the behavioural guard deleted |
|---|---|---|
| **W3** — tolerance in `readString` only | **3 failed** | 2 failed |
| **W2** — dual-read at all five primitive readers | **3 failed** | 2 failed |
| **W2′** — tolerance by normalising the source before any read | 9 failed | 8 failed |

In every case the assertion that disappears when the guard is removed is `reads as nothing -- the reader does not quietly translate it`. It is the only assertion in the file that tests *refusal* rather than *request*, and it is the only one that separates a tolerant reader from a strict one. My W3 and W2 figures match the slice's claim of 3 each.

**And the conclusion behind it is correct — I demonstrated the mechanism.** Whether a key-set observer happens to catch a given dual-read depends entirely on incidental fixture shape. My first two injections were caught observationally, but for the *wrong reason*: because the fixtures omit some asked-for key, the fallback fired and an extra snake_case probe got recorded. That detects the implementation's *probing shape*, not its *tolerance*. So I built **W2′**, which normalises the source once and then reads only camelCase names — fully tolerant, and it records exactly the camelCase key set. Under W2′ the two key-set assertions (`asks the records section…`, `asks the digest section…`) **pass**. The slice's statement — *key-set tests prove the reader asks for the right names; they cannot prove it refuses the wrong ones* — is exactly right, and W2′ is the constructive proof of it.

That is the right lesson to have drawn from a green suite, and drawing it from its own passing tests rather than from a failure is the harder direction.

## The design decision in item 2 — right, and I endorse it

A strict reader that finds nothing draws an empty section, and an empty section is indistinguishable from a case Support has said nothing about. Reporting rather than merely refusing is the correct resolution, it is the same defect AMENDMENT-6 names appearing in a new place, and the slice found it without being asked. The notice names it a fault in the release that composed the panel rather than a fact about the return, so it cannot be read as data — I checked the copy, and the framing is about the release, not the case.

The one-level detection is where it falls short; see F1. That does not disturb the decision, only its reach.

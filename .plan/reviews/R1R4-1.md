## 5. The a11y answer — I confirmed the middle finding, and it is the important one

**(a) and (b) confirmed by inspection**: the only axe lives in a Playwright spec, no CI job runs Playwright, and that sweep visits routes in default state while this token needs `conflict_present: true`.

**(c) confirmed by my own probe, and it is the finding that changes what a future gate can promise.** I ran axe-core under jsdom against `#ffffff` text on a `#ffffff` ground:

```
violations: 0
incomplete: 1
```

**The obvious fix does not work.** jsdom computes no layout or paint, so axe cannot resolve effective colours and downgrades contrast from `violations` to `incomplete`. A vitest a11y test asserting `violations.length === 0` passes on completely invisible text. That means wiring axe into vitest would have produced a green guard over nothing — the exact rule-13 shape, arrived at by doing the sensible thing. The correct instrument for token contrast under vitest is a direct computed-ratio test, which is what both contrast files are, and this retro-justifies `supportContrast.test.ts`'s design as well.

**The new test is genuinely gated.** `npm test` is `vitest run`, which sweeps `src/`; `reviewContrast.test.ts` sits in `src/domains/returns/`; the `frontend-tests` job runs `npm test` on every PR. Rule 13 satisfied.

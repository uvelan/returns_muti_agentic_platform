## 1. Is the field genuinely readable? Yes

A field written but not retrievable is not provenance, so I checked the read side rather
than inferring it from the write:

- `CaseFactView.model_validate` on an actor-stamped document returns `actorId='assoc-7'`;
  on a pre-actor document (key absent entirely) it returns `None`.
- The **projection** carries it, not just the model: injection 1 fails
  `test_the_stored_actor_reads_back_through_the_scoped_projection`, which goes through
  `latest_case_facts_scoped`. Had the field only been assertable on a hand-built model,
  that test would have passed.
- Writing `"actorId"` on **every** scoped write including `None` is the right call and is
  pinned (`test_an_observation_with_no_actor_stores_an_explicit_none`): it makes *no actor*
  and *field absent* distinguishable, which is the difference between provenance and a gap.

## 2. `SCOPED_FACT_IDENTITY_VERSION` not bumped — correct

The version records **which derivation produced `fact_id`**, and the derivation is
untouched: `fact_id` arrives already derived, `append_scoped_case_fact` does not touch it,
and `append_scoped_fact_once` still builds `f"{derived_id}::{record_scope}"` from those two
inputs and nothing else. Bumping it would assert that existing ids came from a different
derivation than they did, and any consumer that keys off the version to choose a derivation
would then mis-handle every pre-1b fact. The reasoning that provenance describes a fact
rather than multiplying it is also right, and it has the stronger property behind it: two
arrivals of one observation under two principals stay one fact, with the first stamp kept
by the same append-once semantics every other field on this path has
(`test_the_same_fact_from_two_actors_is_still_one_fact`).

## 3. Ruling on the deferred REST projection: **deferral is right, not a finding**

`CaseFactProjection` (`case_projection/contract.py:154`) is an explicit allowlist and
`project_facts` (`assembly.py:472`) fills it field by field, so case-level command facts do
reach the REST view without their principal. Three grounds for accepting the deferral, and
the second is the decisive one:

1. **§4's requirement is on the fact, and the fact now carries it.** The durable
   provenance record exists and is readable through the projections that consume it today.
   Rule 6 asks whether a fact lacks its actor; it does not.
2. **`CaseFactProjection` is already an accepted narrowing, and S1 phase 1 set that
   precedent itself.** It omits `turnId`, `correlationId`, and — decisively — phase 1's own
   `record_scope` and `identity_version`, both of which shipped and were reviewed without
   being added to it. Phase 1b is following its own slice's established treatment of a new
   fact field, not inventing an omission.
3. **§3 assigns published-artifact regeneration to the integration agent**, and closing
   this means regenerating five artifacts pinned by `test_openapi_contract_drift.py`.
   Doing that inside a purely-additive S1 fix would be a rule-11 ownership breach for two
   lines of value, and it would widen a six-file diff into a cross-slice one.

**But it must not stay a note.** The moment any surface wants "who authorised this", the
REST view is where it will look, and it will silently answer "nobody". **Orchestrator
action:** dispatch the two-line addition plus regeneration to the **integration agent**,
with the baseline the slice recorded. It is small enough to ride the next artifact
regeneration rather than needing its own round.

## 4. Ruling on the `**fact` trap: **S1 cannot close it — so it becomes a condition on the two adoption dispatches**

The trap is real, and I reproduced it rather than reasoning about it:

```
append_scoped_fact_once params:      ['self', 'record_scope', 'actor_id', 'fact']   <- explicit
ScopedFactAppendPort.__call__ params: ['self', 'record_scope', 'fact']              <- absorbs it
double captured keys:                 ['actor_id', 'case_id', 'fact_name']
production stores top-level key:      'actorId'
```

A consuming slice's double written against the port captures `actor_id` inside `**fact`,
under the **snake_case** key, while production stores top-level `actorId`. A double
asserting `fact["actor_id"] == "assoc-7"` passes whether or not production stored anything.
Given this run's history — V1 phase 2's `**fact` double hid five dead fact writes — that is
exactly the shape that has already cost a round.

**S1 genuinely cannot close it.** No signature S1 writes stops a consumer's `**kwargs` from
absorbing a keyword; the doubles live in the consuming slices and only they can bind the
parameter. S1 did the part that is reachable from here: it asserts the **stored** document
through the real repository (`test_the_activity_stamps_the_actor_into_the_stored_document`),
which is the assertion the consumers should copy. So this is not a finding against S1.

**But "flagged to consumers" is thinner than it looks, and I am converting it into an
acceptance condition rather than leaving it in a ledger.** Both dispatched one-liners —
V3 (`operations/return_support/clarification.py` ~172) and V1 phase 2
(`operations/support_template_gate.py::record_revision` ~650) — must, as a condition of
their review:

- assert the **stored** `actorId` (top-level, camelCase) through a repository double or
  fake collection, **not** `fact["actor_id"]` captured through a `**fact` signature;
- bind `actor_id` explicitly in whatever double they use, so the parameter cannot be
  absorbed silently;
- and, since both also delete a value-level key (`"answeredBy"`, and V1's stopgap), assert
  that the old key is **gone** from the stored value — otherwise the two spellings coexist
  and the migration has not actually happened.

I will check those three things specifically when those two changes come back.

**Advisory, for the slice's judgement:** the stated reason for leaving the Protocol alone —
"narrowing it isn't additive" — looks wrong to me. Adding a keyword-only parameter with a
default to a Protocol is accepted by every existing `**fact` implementer, so it appears
additive in the sense that matters. It would not close the trap (a `**fact` double still
absorbs it), but it would put `actor_id` in the contract a consumer reads while writing
that double, which is where the mistake is made. Cheap, and worth considering; not a
finding, and not a reason to hold this branch.

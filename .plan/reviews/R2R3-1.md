## 5. The two adjudications

### (a) Removing both empty-reply entries from `ABSENCE_VOCABULARY` — **a correct trade, and better than claimed**

The concern is real: the fabrication walk notes literals, not identifiers, so replacing
the sentences with a shared constant genuinely removes those two positions from the walk's
view. The question is what happens to the mutation the vocabulary used to catch — someone
changing that sentence into a fabrication.

I tested it. Changing `EMPTY_REPLY_NOTICE`'s **value** to `"Delivered on time to Support."`
produces a new failure: **`is present tense and names the next action`**. So the position is
not unwatched; it is watched by a *property* assertion instead of by set membership — and a
property assertion is the stronger instrument, because vocabulary membership only asks
"is this exact string on a list" while this asks something true of the sentence.

Add the server-side refusal, which makes the wording no longer load-bearing for safety at
all, and the trade is net positive. Removing the entries also avoids exactly what the
vocabulary is meant not to accumulate: an allowlist line that matches nothing, which is the
line someone later deletes without understanding — or worse, keeps and reasons from.

**Not a loss.** The one thing genuinely given up (walk visibility of those two positions)
is replaced by something that binds harder.

### (b) Not adding `EmptyReplyBodyError` to `_auto_send_or_reason`'s except tuple — **right, with the trap to be named at the site**

Confirmed by inspection: the tuple lists `ReviewConflictError`, `PendingRevisionError`,
`ReviewVersionMismatchError`, `ReviewStateError`, and `EmptyReplyBodyError` appears nowhere
in `return_case_activities.py`.

**The decision is right.** An `except` entry for a raise that cannot occur is untestable —
nobody could verify the reason string, and it would read as coverage while being dead code.
That is rule 13's own principle in the error-handling plane: a handler with no reachable
raise is a comment. It also presumes a design for reply auto-send that does not exist.

**And the failure direction is the safe one**, which is what settles it: if reply auto-send
is added without extending the tuple, `EmptyReplyBodyError` propagates out of the activity —
an activity failure, retried and surfaced. Loud, not an empty send.

**But the trap is currently recorded only in the ledger, and the person who will hit it
reads `_auto_send_or_reason`.** Its docstring even says *"Every refusal below is a guard
block"*, which reply auto-send would quietly falsify. One comment line at the site closes
it. Advisory A1 — not a finding, because the shipped behaviour is correct and the failure
is loud.

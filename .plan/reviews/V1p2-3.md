### The identity assertion — the right instrument, with one boundary worth naming

`test_both_surfaces_read_the_same_temporal_answer_the_same_way` does two things, and it needs both. It asserts **identity of the function object** on each surface (`case_panel.classify_execution_failure is classify_execution_failure`, same for `case_reviews`), and it pins the classifier's **full truth table** — `NOT_FOUND → ABSENT`; `UNAVAILABLE`/`DEADLINE_EXCEEDED`/`TimeoutError`/`ConnectionError` → `UNREACHABLE`; `PERMISSION_DENIED` and `ValueError` → `None`.

Identity is the better half, and the stated reason is right: comparing outcomes at two call sites would pass again the moment somebody added a third status to only one of them, because both would still agree on whatever statuses the test happened to enumerate. Identity makes divergence *unrepresentable* rather than *detected on the cases someone thought of*. Pairing it with the table is what stops "they agree" degenerating into "they agree about nothing in particular" — and it is why all three classifier injections above turned this test red.

**The boundary, for the record and not as a finding:** identity catches a second *copy*, not a *wrapper*. The panel legitimately keeps `TimeoutError → EXECUTION_QUERY_TIMEOUT` ahead of the classifier, and identity would still hold if that pre-empt ever grew. That specific pre-empt is correct — the same classification said more precisely for a surface that renders a reason an operator reads, while the endpoint only needs the retry decision, which is unchanged. But it shows what the instrument does and does not cover, and a future third surface should be held to the same "finer, never different" standard.
